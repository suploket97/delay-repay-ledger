How the estimate works

  • Delay is how late you arrived at your destination, not how late the train left.
  • Claim from the company whose train caused the first delay, even if you bought through Trainline or another retailer.
  • Single: 25% / 50% / 100% / 100% of the fare for 15, 30, 60 and 120+ minutes. Return (one leg late): 12.5% / 25% / 50% / 100% of the return fare.
  • Season tickets: one journey is 1/10 of a weekly, 1/40 of a monthly, 1/120 of a quarterly or 1/464 of an annual; doubled at 120+ minutes.
  • Most operators want the claim within 28 days of travel. Keep a photo of the ticket.

First Class and Seatfrog

  • The percentage is the same in both classes; it applies to the higher First Class fare, so the payout is larger.
  • If First Class wasn't available, ask the operator separately for the difference between First and Standard fares.
  • Seatfrog upgrades qualify for Delay Repay. Claim from the train company, not Seatfrog, and attach the upgrade confirmation. The £3 platform fee is never refunded.
  • Upgrade not scanned because the train was cancelled? Ask Seatfrog for a refund of the unused upgrade instead.

Didn't travel?

  • That's a refund, not Delay Repay. Ask the retailer that sold the ticket.
  • Since 1 April 2026, refunds for unused walk-up tickets normally have to be asked for by 23:59 the day before the ticket is valid, but disruption is an exception.
  • If you took a later train and arrived late, that is Delay Repay.

This is a personal note-taking tool. It estimates compensation from published scheme rules and does not submit claims. Operators make the final decision; split tickets, rovers, pay-as-you-go and carnets can be worked out differently. Your journeys are stored only on this device, in this browser.
